Start here: hardware safety

Updated 28 October 2026. General guidance, not professional electrical advice.

Golden rule: everything in these guides runs on low voltage only — USB power (5 V) or small batteries (3–9 V). Never connect a project to mains power (240 V wall sockets). Mains can kill. Leave it to a licensed electrician — in Australia, unlicensed mains wiring is illegal.

The short list

  • Low voltage only. USB from a computer or phone charger, AA/AAA packs, or a 9 V battery. Nothing from the wall except a normal USB charger.
  • Unplug before you change wiring. Move wires with the power off, then check before plugging back in.
  • No short circuits. Never join power (5V / 3V3 / VIN / +) straight to ground (GND / −). Wires get hot, batteries swell, and boards die.
  • Use a resistor with every LED. Usually 220 Ω–1 kΩ. Without one the LED (or the pin) burns out.
  • Check pin voltage. ESP32 and Raspberry Pi Pico pins are 3.3 V — feeding them 5 V can destroy them. Classic Arduino Uno pins are 5 V.
  • If anything gets hot, smells or smokes — unplug straight away. Let it cool, then find the mistake.
  • Batteries: never short, crush, puncture or charge lithium (LiPo / 18650) cells without a proper charger board with protection. Don't leave charging batteries unattended. Recycle used batteries — never in the household bin.
  • Soldering (optional): irons run at 300 °C+. Use a stand, work in fresh air, wash hands after (solder can contain lead — choose lead-free), and wear safety glasses when trimming leads.

Kids and learners

  • Under-12s: an adult does the plugging-in and any soldering. Breadboards (no solder) are best.
  • Keep button/coin batteries and small magnets away from little ones — swallowing one is a medical emergency (call 000, or the Poisons Information Centre on 13 11 26).
  • Do builds on a clear, dry table, away from drinks.

What these guides won't cover

  • Mains-powered or high-voltage projects (wall power, mains relays switching lights or heaters, microwave parts, tasers, coil guns).
  • Anything that jams, intercepts or spies — radio jammers, hidden cameras or recording people without consent, tracking people without permission. These are illegal in Australia.
  • Drones or vehicles beyond basic toys — CASA rules apply.

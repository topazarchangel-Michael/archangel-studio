Schematics & blueprints

A schematic shows how parts connect, not where they sit. A blueprint (or wiring plan) adds the physical layout — which breadboard row, which pin.

Common symbols

 Battery / supply   ─┤|─      (long line = +)
 Ground             ⏚  or  GND
 Resistor           ─[ R ]─   (value in Ω, kΩ)
 LED                ─▶|─      (arrow points + to −)
 Button             ─o o─
 Capacitor          ─||─      (electrolytic: watch the − stripe)
 Junction           ──┬──     (wires joined)

Reading one, step by step

  1. Find power and ground first — every part connects back to GND.
  2. Follow each line from a board pin to the part and on to GND or power.
  3. Check every LED has a resistor, and nothing joins power straight to GND.
  4. Note the voltage: 3.3 V parts on 3.3 V boards.

From schematic to breadboard

 Breadboard: rows of 5 holes are joined across; the long rails down the sides are + and −.

   + rail ─────────────────────  (3V3)
   − rail ─────────────────────  (GND)
   row 10: GPIO15 wire │ 220Ω leg
   row 14: 220Ω leg    │ LED long leg
   row 16: LED short leg ── jumper to − rail

A device blueprint template

Copy this into your project notes:

 Device name:
 Board:            (Pico / ESP32 / Uno)   Logic: 3.3 V / 5 V
 Power:            USB 5 V / battery pack (no mains)
 Inputs:           e.g. button GPIO14, light sensor ADC26
 Outputs:          e.g. LED GPIO15, servo GPIO13
 Link to app:      Web Serial / Web Bluetooth / Wi-Fi
 Messages:         board → app: "temp:22.4"   app → board: "led:on"
 Safety checks:    resistor on every LED · no 5 V on 3.3 V pins · no short circuits

Free tools for drawing them

  • Wokwi — simulate and share wiring in the browser.
  • KiCad — free, open-source schematic and circuit-board design.
  • Fritzing — friendly breadboard-style diagrams (small fee for downloads; source is open).
  • Archangel's Builder → Blueprint tab draws your app's schematic; pair it with a device blueprint above.

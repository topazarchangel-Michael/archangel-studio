Quantum computing — learn it by building

Real physics, honestly explained. Every app here is a classical simulation that runs on your phone — great for learning, not a real quantum computer.

What a qubit actually is

  • A normal bit is 0 or 1. A qubit has two complex numbers, α (for 0) and β (for 1), with |α|² + |β|² = 1.
  • When you measure, you get 0 with probability |α|² and 1 with probability |β|². Then the qubit *becomes* that answer.
  • "In two states at once" is a handy shortcut, but the real idea is amplitudes that can cancel or add up (interference). That is where quantum speed-ups come from.

Bell states and entanglement

  1. Put qubit 0 through H.
  2. CNOT from qubit 0 to qubit 1.
  3. Measure 1024 times: you only ever see 00 or 11, roughly half each. The results are linked — but you still can't send a message faster than light with it.

How the simulator maths works (for builders)

  • n qubits → a list of 2ⁿ complex numbers (the state vector). 4 qubits = 16 numbers, easy on a phone. 25+ qubits gets too big for a browser.
  • A one-qubit gate on qubit k: for every pair of indexes that differ only in bit k, multiply the pair by the 2×2 matrix.
  • CNOT: for every index where the control bit is 1, swap the amplitudes for target = 0 and target = 1.
  • Measure: pick a random number 0–1, walk through the probabilities |aᵢ|² until you pass it.

Keep it honest — red flags

  • "Quantum" healing, energy pendants, manifesting or "quantum" trading bots are marketing, not physics.
  • Today's real quantum computers are small and noisy. They're useful for research, not for breaking your bank's encryption tomorrow.

Go further (free)

  • IBM Quantum Learning and Qiskit — run tiny circuits on real hardware for free.
  • Quirk (algassert.com/quirk) — a drag-and-drop circuit simulator in the browser.
  • Microsoft Quantum Katas — practice exercises with instant checks.
  • Year 11–12 physics or first-year linear algebra (vectors and matrices) is the best foundation.
